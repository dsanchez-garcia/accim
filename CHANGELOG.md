## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **`accim.sim.pmv_index`, one engine for the setpoints based on indices derived from the PMV**: `apply_pmv_index_setpoints(building, index, index_parameters, ...)` adds the thermal comfort thermostats (Fanger, dual setpoint) and the EMS programs of the setpoints of an index of `PMV_INDICES` (`'apmv'`, `'epmv'` and `'pmv'`). The indices share all the EMS objects except the lines that depend on the index (`PmvIndex`): the coefficient of each season, the inverse of the index (the PMV setpoint of the thermostat whose index is the setpoint of the index) and the index of each zone. `apply_apmv_setpoints()` calls it with the index `'apmv'`: its output IDFs do not change (golden master). The functions stop with a `ValueError` if the model already has the setpoints of another index, since both would actuate the same thermostats. Section 4.8 of the detailed use.
- **`accim.sim.apmv.apply_pmv_setpoints()`**: setpoints based on the PMV of Fanger, such as those of the categories of ISO 7730 and EN 16798-1, with the arguments of `apply_apmv_setpoints()` except the adaptive coefficients. They are the setpoints of `apply_apmv_setpoints()` with adaptive coefficients of 0, with EMS objects named after the PMV: the programs `apply_PMV_<zone>` and `count_PMV_comfort_hours_<zone>`, and the outputs `PMV_<zone>` (the PMV of the zone, the average of its occupants in the zones with several), `PMV Heating Setpoint_<zone>`, `PMV Cooling Setpoint_<zone>`... and the hours within the setpoints. Section 4.8 of the detailed use.
- **`accim.sim.apmv.apply_epmv_setpoints()`**: setpoints based on the PMV with the expectancy factor (ePMV) of Fanger and Toftum (2002), ePMV = e·PMV, for buildings without air-conditioning in warm climates (the index `'epmv'` of `accim.sim.pmv_index`). The expectancy factor `expectancy_factor` of each zone (0.8 by default; a `ValueError` out of 0.5 to 1) applies in the cooling season and only to the warm sensations, since it only affects the upper limit of the comfort zone: the PMV setpoint of the cooling thermostat is the ePMV setpoint divided by e, the heating setpoint is a PMV setpoint, and e is 1 in the heating season; the ePMV of each zone (the output `ePMV_<zone>`, with which the hours within the setpoints are counted) is e·PMV when the PMV is positive, and the PMV otherwise. `apply_to_heating=True` also applies e to the heating setpoint and to the cool sensations, with a warning. Outputs `ePMV_<zone>`, `Expectancy Factor_<zone>`, the setpoints and the hours. Section 4.8.1 of the detailed use.
- **The metabolic correction of the ePMV** (`apply_epmv_setpoints(metabolic_correction=True)`): in the cooling season, the activity level of the occupants is reduced by 6.7 % for each unit of PMV above neutral, up to 3, with the PMV of the previous timestep (EMS programs `correct_activity_<people>`, run after those of the zones). The activity level schedule of each People object becomes a copy of it that the EMS sets (`ePMV_Activity_<people>`, a `Schedule:Compact`, `Schedule:Constant`, `Schedule:Year` or `Schedule:File`), and an EMS sensor reads the original one; the outputs `Metabolic Correction Factor_<people>` and `Corrected Activity Level_<people>` report them. The correction tends to the activity level whose PMV gives it, slightly less than that of Fanger and Toftum (5.5 to 6.1 % instead of 6.7 % for a PMV of 1 with 1 to 1.2 met), and it also reduces the heat gains of the occupants, from the next timestep; a People object of several zones is corrected with the mean PMV of its occupants in them, with a warning. `set_pmv_input_parameters()` stops with a `ValueError` if it is asked to set the activity level of a People object with the correction (set it before applying the setpoints). Section 4.8.1 of the detailed use.
- **`accim.constants.EPMV_EXPECTANCY_RANGES` and `EPMV_MET_REDUCTION_PER_PMV`**: the ranges of the expectancy factor of Fanger and Toftum (2002, Table 1: high expectation 0.9 to 1, moderate 0.7 to 0.9, low 0.5 to 0.7) and the reduction of the metabolic rate (0.067 for each unit of PMV).
- **The ePMV setpoints in the parametric module**: the parameters type `'epmv setpoints'` applies `apply_epmv_setpoints()` with its default values (unless `bypass_add_accis=True`), and its parameters are `expectancy_factor` (`param_apmv.change_expectancy_factor_all_zones()`), `pmv_cooling_setpoint`, `pmv_heating_setpoint` and `pmv_setpoint` (`accim.parametric_and_optimisation.parameters.get_available_params_epmv_setpoints()`).

PMV-based setpoints* (*2. aPMV setpoints* before) has the index of the setpoints (`apmv`, `epmv` or `pmv`), the expectancy factor and the options of the ePMV (`apply_to_heating`, `metabolic_correction`); the fields of the other indices are disabled, also in the table of the zones, which has a field for the expectancy factor. The new IDF is `<input>_aPMV.idf`, `<input>_ePMV.idf` or `<input>_PMV.idf` by default. Section 4.10 of the detailed use.

Only the output IDFs whose zones need it have these objects: those of the other comfort standards, and those of the custom model with `'pmot'`, do not change (golden master). - **The minimum heating setpoint and the maximum cooling setpoint**: `heating_setpoint_minimum` and `cooling_setpoint_maximum` of `AddAccis()`, of `modify_accis()` and of `zone_arguments` (°C, `None` by default) bound the setpoints of the ACCIS, whatever the comfort standard and the comfort mode: the heating setpoint is not lower than the minimum, and the cooling setpoint not higher than the maximum. Only the output IDFs with a minimum or a maximum have these objects: the others do not change (golden master). ## [1.1.0] - 2026-11-08

Version 1.1.0: `accim.sim` restructured around eppy, so that its code is easier to understand: accim reads, changes and writes the models with the methods of eppy instead of code of its own, and no longer reads them with besos. ### Changed
- **The output IDFs of `AddAccis` only have the EMS code of the comfort standards of their zones** (the comfort standard of the output IDF, and those of `zone_arguments`): the programs `SetComfTemp`, `SetAppLimits`, `ApplyCAT`, `CountHours_<zone>` and `SetAST_Master` keep their branches of these comfort standards, and the subprograms `SetAST_CS<n>_<chunk>` are those of these comfort standards only (`accim.sim.ems.programs.set_comfort_standard_code()`); before, they had the code of the 24 comfort standards. They do the same as before (checked for every comfort standard, category and comfort mode with the Erl compiler of accim, and with EnergyPlus). An output IDF of the sample model with two zones in mixed mode goes from 78 EMS programs with 6851 lines (645 KB) to 34 programs with 1393 lines (357 KB). The comfort standard of an output IDF can no longer be changed in its programs `SetInputData_<zone>` (EnergyPlus stops: the setpoints of a comfort standard without its code are not set), but `modify_accis()` adds the code of the comfort standards that it sets, and so does `modify_ComfStand()` of the parametric module (`accim.sim.ems.programs.add_comfort_standard_code()`), for instance with an output IDF and `bypass_add_accis=True`; the category, the comfort mode and the other arguments can still be changed there. The models of `add_accis_to_idf()`, and so those of the parametric module, still have the code of all the comfort standards. The argument `comf_stands` of `accim.sim.ems.setast.subprograms()` is now `comfort_standards`, as that of `master_program_lines()`.
- **The lines of the EMS programs are indented** inside the blocks `IF ... ENDIF` and `WHILE ... ENDWHILE` (4 spaces for each block), so that it can be seen where each block begins and ends: `add_program()` and `set_program_lines()` indent the lines that they write, and `AddAccis` and `modify_accis()` indent all the EMS programs and subroutines of the model, because eppy removes the spaces at the beginning of the fields when it reads an IDF (`accim.sim.ems.programs.indent_lines()` and `indent_programs()`). EnergyPlus ignores these spaces: the results do not change.
- **The models are read with eppy, not besos**: `accim.sim.accis.load_idf()` reads every model with eppy, also with `energyplus_version='auto'`, which now takes the IDD of the EnergyPlus version of the `Version` object of the model, where accim finds it with an explicit version (`accim.energyplus.idd_path()`, with the environment variable `ACCIM_ENERGYPLUS_DIR`); if that version is not installed, it stops with a `FileNotFoundError`. `AddAccis` (whose `input_idfs` and `output_idfs` are IDF objects of eppy), `Table` (which no longer imports besos), `apply_heating_activation_time_sch()` (`accim.sim.chile_funcs` no longer imports besos), `run_simulation()` of the Python API backend, `verify_accim_simulation()`, `AccimSimulationVerifier`, `modify_timesteps_path()`, `set_occupancy_to_always_path()` and `set_operative_temp_control(idf_path=...)` read the models with it. eppy reads all the models of a Python session with one IDD, so a model of another EnergyPlus version stops with a `ValueError` that says to use a new Python session (with `'auto'`, besos read it with the IDD that it already had and only warned). The functions that receive an IDF object (`add_accis_to_idf()`, `modify_accis()`, `apply_apmv_setpoints()`... and the parametric module) still accept those of besos.
- **The output IDFs are saved by eppy**: for each combination of arguments, `AddAccis` sets them in the programs `SetInputData_<zone>` of the model (`accim.sim.generation.set_combination()`) and saves it with eppy (`IDF.savecopy()`). It is slower than the writer of accim 1.0.0, which wrote once the text of the objects that do not change (`AddAccis` with the 140 output IDFs of the sample model of `test_generation.py`: about 5 s with 1.0.0, 16 s now), but it is plain eppy.
- **Some numbers of the models are written as eppy writes them** in the output IDFs of `AddAccis`: eppy reads the numeric fields as numbers, so `2.0` is written `2`, `0.00000001` `1e-08` and `-0` `0`; besos kept them as text. The values do not change, and neither do the results of the simulations (checked with EnergyPlus).
- `accim.sim` uses the methods of eppy instead of helpers of its own: `IDF.getobject()` (an object by its name, in any case), `IDF.removeallidfobjects()`, `IDF.idfstr()`, `IDF.savecopy()`, `EpBunch.fieldvalues`... An object of accim whose name is already in the model with other upper and lower case is no longer added again (EnergyPlus takes the names of the objects as case-insensitive).
- The Python API backend reads and changes the models with eppy instead of parsing the text of the IDF: `accim.sim.energyplus_api.read_ems()` takes an IDF object of eppy, and `prepare_for_api()` removes its EMS programs, program calling managers and actuators, and eppy saves it. In a Python session, `run_simulation()` runs the models of one EnergyPlus version, as the other functions that read models with eppy; `run_simulations()` and `run_ep(backend='api')` run each simulation in its own process, as before.
- `apply_apmv_setpoints()` and `set_pmv_input_parameters()` write their EMS programs and schedules from lists, and the lines of the cooling season are those of `SetAST_Master` (`accim.sim.ems.setast.COOLING_SEASON_LINES`); the objects they add are the same.
- **The windows of mixed mode are found in any model, and only accim opens and closes them** (`vrf_mm` and `ex_mm`, `accim.sim.windows`): with an AirflowNetwork that simulates the multizone airflows, the windows, doors and glass doors of the exterior surfaces of the occupied zones whose `AirflowNetwork:MultiZone:Surface` objects have an opening, whatever their names (before, only those named as in DesignBuilder, `..._Win` and `..._Door`, with the zone before the first `_`; the doors between zones keep the control of the model); otherwise (also with `NoMultizoneOrDistribution` and `MultizoneWithDistributionOnlyDuringFanOperation`, with which EnergyPlus simulates the `ZoneVentilation` objects instead), the `ZoneVentilation:WindandStackOpenArea` objects and the `ZoneVentilation:DesignFlowRate` objects of the type `Natural` of the occupied zones (the fans, `Exhaust`, `Intake` and `Balanced`, are no longer taken as windows), also those of spaces, ZoneLists and SpaceLists (replaced with one object per zone or space). Their own limits (minimum and maximum indoor and outdoor temperatures, delta temperature, maximum wind speed) are removed, since the EMS decides when they ventilate: they kept closed the windows that it opened (the first window of the OSM residential model of the golden master never ventilated, its maximum indoor temperature being a schedule of 1 ?C). The occupant ventilation controls of the windows (`AirflowNetwork:OccupantVentilationControl`; that of a zone, which EnergyPlus gives to all its windows and doors, is given only to those that accim does not control) and the `AvailabilityManager:HybridVentilation` objects are removed with a warning when accim controls windows, as they override the EMS (the hybrid ventilation managers act on all the `ZoneVentilation` objects of the zones of their air loops, not only on the one they name); the hybrid ventilation managers stay when the AirflowNetwork simulates the air distribution system together with the windows (`MultizoneWithDistribution`), since EnergyPlus may not solve open windows while the fans run. Warnings list the occupied zones without windows, the EMS actuators of the model on the windows that accim controls, and the models with `MultizoneWithDistributionOnlyDuringFanOperation`, whose `ZoneVentilation` objects EnergyPlus only simulates while the fans are off. The names of the windows in the EMS objects are valid and unique (any character but letters, digits and `_` becomes `_`).
- **In mixed mode (`vrf_mm` and `ex_mm`), the zones are air-conditioned on the design days**, with the windows closed, whether they are occupied or not (the EMS sensor `DesignDay`, of the new schedule `ACCIS Design Days`): the HVAC systems were off on the design days without occupants (often the winter design day) and while the windows were open, so EnergyPlus sized them for no load or a lower one. With `ex_mm`, it stopped (`... has air flow less than ...`, `Autosizing of heating coil UA failed`), or the systems could not heat; with `vrf_mm` and the sample model, whose winter design day has no occupants, the heating design loads of its zones were 0 (EnergyPlus warned `Calculated design heating load for zone=... is zero`), and its cooling design loads, and so its VRF systems, are now about 6 % lower (the peak no longer includes the pull-down after the hours without occupants). The output IDFs of `vrf_mm` and `ex_mm` change.
- **The thermostats of ZoneLists are split** into one thermostat per zone (`<zone> <thermostat>`, as EnergyPlus names them), with their operative temperature controls, `ZoneControl:Thermostat:TemperatureAndHumidity` and `FaultModel:ThermostatOffset` objects and the `DemandManager:Thermostats` that name them, and so are the staged thermostats (`ZoneControl:Thermostat:StagedDualSetpoint`) of ZoneLists: before, accim stopped with a `ValueError` for the thermostats of ZoneLists.
- **With `existing_hvac_operative_temperature_control=True`**, the thermal comfort thermostats (`ZoneControl:Thermostat:ThermalComfort`, PMV of Fanger) of the occupied zones are removed with a warning (EnergyPlus used them instead of the setpoints of accim, and crashed with the example file `FurnaceWithDXSystemComfortControl`), and the staged thermostats get the setpoint schedules of their zones (the multispeed systems with staged control heated and cooled with their own setpoints); with `False`, a warning.
- **The pages of the How-to Guides were generated again with accim 1.1.0**: `full_example_IBPSA` and `using_apmv_setpoints` show the EMS programs of 1.1.0 (an output IDF of the sample model has 34 programs instead of 78, and their lines are indented). The results of the full example change slightly, only because of the design days of `vrf_mm` (without them, they are the same as in the pages of 1.0.0): for instance, the heating demand of `BRA_Stat_MM` goes from 130.42 to 130.20 kWh/m2. `using_addAccis`, `using_runEp` and `research_paper_case_study` were also generated again (they printed the banner of 1.0.0, and the first two show the new functions, see Added); the results of the research paper case study change slightly with the changes of mixed mode of 1.1.0 (`vrf_mm`, below): the total energy demand of `Ind_Adap_MM` in Shimla goes from 89.67 to 89.59 kWh/m2, and `AddAccis` warns that its core zone has no windows that accim can open.
- **The arguments of each zone in the graphical user interface**: the table of `zone_arguments` of the tab *Add the ACCIS* is right below the comfort models (it was at the end of the tab, below the existing HVAC systems and the tolerances, where it went unnoticed); its main arguments (comfort standard, category, comfort mode and, in mixed mode, HVAC mode and ventilation control) are chosen in lists, those of the categories and comfort modes being those of the comfort standard of the zone, and the others are written as pairs `name=value`, as before; *Preview* and *Add the ACCIS* check that each zone has setpoints in all the output IDFs, as `AddAccis` does after preparing the models. *Add the ACCIS* asks whether to write the output IDFs once `AddAccis` has prepared the models and estimated the size of their results, as it asks in the prompt (before, the window asked before running it), and the buttons of the tabs *Add the ACCIS* and *aPMV setpoints* stay at the bottom of the tab. When eppy cannot read an IDF of another EnergyPlus version, the window explains how to open it again.

### Fixed

## [1.0.0] - 2026-11-03

Version 1.0.0: a refactoring of the package to the conventions of Python (PEP 8), faster, split into smaller modules, and with a new backend that runs the simulations through the Python API of EnergyPlus. The scripts written for accim 0.x still work: the old names are deprecated aliases (see the migration guide, `docs/source/9_migration_guide.md`). The usage of `accim.parametric_and_optimisation` does not change, apart from the names of its parameters and of the arguments of its classes, which are now those of `AddAccis` and `modify_accis` (the old ones still work); its results only change for the models whose natural ventilation is given by `ZoneVentilation` objects and for the optimisations with OMOPSO, SMPSO, CMAES, GeneticAlgorithm and EvolutionaryStrategy, the Pareto status of the optimisations with constraints takes them into account, the full-set plans of the predefined models no longer keep some invalid combinations and keep ESP CTE (see Changed), and the post-processing follows the calendar of EnergyPlus (the daily and monthly tables, and the year of the hourly tables without `start_date`) and stops with clear errors where it gave wrong values per m2 (see Fixed).

### Added
- **Different arguments for each zone**: the new argument `zone_arguments` of `AddAccis` and `modify_accis` gives the arguments of some occupied zones, `{zone: {argument: value}}` (for instance, `{'Block1:Zone2': {'comfort_standard': 2, 'category': 80, 'comfort_mode': 3, 'hvac_mode': 0}}`): the comfort standard, category, comfort mode, HVAC mode, ventilation control, tolerance (`setpoint_tolerance`), the arguments of the custom model and the others of `modify_accis`, one value each. The zones are given by their names (in any case) or by the keys of the aPMV setpoints ('<space> <people>'). With `AddAccis`, these zones have the same arguments in all the output IDFs, whose names give the arguments of the other zones, and accim checks before writing any output IDF that each of them has setpoints in all the combinations; it needs `setpoint_type='temperature'`. With `modify_accis`, the arguments that are `None` keep the values of each zone. New outputs of the setpoints of each zone: `Comfort Temperature_<zone>`, `Adaptive Cooling Setpoint Temperature_<zone>`, `Adaptive Heating Setpoint Temperature_<zone>`, their values without tolerance and, in mixed mode, `Ventilation Setpoint Temperature_<zone>` and `Minimum Outdoor Temperature for ventilation_<zone>`; the outputs of the whole model keep their names and have the values of the last zone (those of every zone when all the zones have the same arguments). The models of earlier versions of accim, whose zones share one program `SetInputData`, keep working with `modify_accis` and the parametric module, with the same arguments in all the zones.
- **Graphical user interface (GUI)** in English (`accim.gui`, with tkinter, which comes with Python): the command `accim-gui` (or `python -m accim.gui`) opens a window with seven tabs that share a working folder: *Add the ACCIS* (the input IDFs of the folder and the arguments of `AddAccis`, with the fields that do not apply to the script type, the setpoint type or the comfort standards disabled, the valid categories and comfort modes of the selected standards, a preview of the number of output IDFs, the custom model, the options of the existing HVAC systems (`existing_hvac_*`, with `ex_ac` and `ex_mm`), and a table with the arguments of some zones, `zone_arguments`, whose occupied zones it finds in the IDF), *aPMV setpoints* (`apply_apmv_setpoints` on an IDF of the folder, with the same setpoints in all the zones, optionally with VRF systems, the occupants always present and other inputs of the PMV), *Rename the EPWs* (`rename_epw_files`, with the cities and countries that OpenStreetMap does not give), *Run the simulations* (`run_ep` with either backend, and `remove_files`), *Hours within the setpoints* (the percentages of each simulation, those that reach a minimum, and a CSV file), *Table and figures* (`Table` of the CSV files of the folder, exported to CSV, saved and opened again (see the sessions below), and its scatter plots and time plots, saved as PNG files) and *Parametric study* (a basic `ParametricSimulation` of the predefined or the custom model, also with the existing HVAC systems, `ex_ac`, and their options: the full set of some options or a Latin hypercube sample of some ranges, with the sums of some meters as results), and a menu *Session* that saves the session and opens it again (see below). The tasks run in the background, one at a time, with what accim prints in a log; the forms are checked before running (`accim.gui.forms` and the tasks of `accim.gui.tasks` do not need a display), and the GUI never waits for an answer in the prompt (it only answers the questions of `rename_epw_files` about amending or excluding EPWs, and those of the figures about renaming the rows and columns of the subplots). Not covered: the `custom` output type, different aPMV setpoints for each zone, the other figures of `Table`, and the outputs, objectives, optimisations and plots of the parametric module. Section 4.10 of the detailed use; tests in `tests/core/test_gui.py`.
- **Sessions saved in files, to resume them**: `Table.save()` saves a table of `Table` in a pickle file, which `Table.load()` reads in a new session (for instance, after restarting the kernel of a notebook) without reading the CSV files of the simulations again, and in a JSON file with the same name, which people and other programs can read: the version of accim, the date and the working folder, and the arguments of the table, its frequency, zones and blocks, the variables whose values differ between the simulations, the columns given by `format_table`, and the number of rows and the first 1000 rows (`json_max_rows`) of its table and of the tables of `wrangled_table`. `accim.utils.save_session()` and `load_session()` do the same with several objects at once (tables, DataFrames, lists of files, arguments...), as the parametric module with its results. `Table` keeps its arguments (`Table.arguments`) and those of the last `format_table` (`type_of_table`, `custom_cols`). In the GUI, the menu *Session* saves the working folder, the values of the forms of all the tabs and the results of the tabs *Hours within the setpoints*, *Table and figures* and *Parametric study* in these files, and opens them again; *Table and figures* also saves its table and opens tables, including those saved in Python. The notebook of the research paper case study saves and resumes its tables. Section 4.7.2 of the detailed use; tests in `tests/core/test_session.py` and `tests/core/test_gui.py`.
- **Hours within the setpoints** (`accim.data.postprocessing.setpoint_hours`): `hours_within_setpoints()` gives the percentage of the occupied hours within the setpoints (without tolerance) of each zone and of the building, from the CSV files of the simulations (one row for each file, whatever the frequency of the outputs) or from a table with one row for each simulation and the sums of the EMS outputs `Occupied Comfortable Hours_No Applicability_<zone>` and `Occupied Hours_<zone>` (the results of the parametric module, for instance); `filter_by_hours_within_setpoints()` keeps the simulations whose percentage reaches a minimum in the building, in every zone or in some zones, for instance to give `Table` only their CSV files, or to leave out the evaluations of a parametric study whose HVAC system or natural ventilation does not keep the setpoints. Section 4.7.1 of the detailed use describes the hour counters of the ACCIS, and the overview page of the parametric module how to read them.
- **Python API backend**: `run_ep(backend='api')` runs the simulations through the Python API of EnergyPlus (`pyenergyplus`, EnergyPlus 24.2 or later) instead of its Energy Management System (EMS). EnergyPlus runs each model without its EMS programs, program calling managers and actuators, and accim runs the programs in the callbacks of the API with the rules of the EMS of EnergyPlus (`accim.sim.ems.erl`: the order in which the operators are grouped, the minus sign, the values Null and uninitialised (an operator whose operands are not both numbers gives the number 0 without setting it), `^` as `pow` of C, the errors while sizing and afterwards, the reset of the variables at each new environment, the actuators released when Null, the integer actuators rounded down and the logical ones true only for 1 (the Python API itself rounds the integers to the nearest one and takes as true the numbers within 1e-5 of 1, so the controller finds the type of each actuator), the messages of `@WarnEP`, `@SevereWarnEP` and `@FatalHaltEP` in the error file, the scope of the variables that EMS output variables report...). The results are identical to those of the EMS: all the model types of accim (VRF and existing HVAC systems, fully air-conditioned and mixed mode, temperature and PMV control, aPMV setpoints, EnergyPlus 25.1 to 26.2) give the same CSV files, also over a whole year, in the same time. `accim.sim.energyplus_api.run_simulation()` runs one model, and its hooks (`before_programs`, `after_programs`) can read and change the variables of the programs during the simulation; `run_simulations()` runs several models in parallel, each in its own process. Not supported (with a clear error): `EnergyManagementSystem:TrendVariable`, `CurveOrTableIndexVariable` and `ConstructionIndexVariable`, the external interface, Python plugins, the calling point `UserDefinedComponentModel`, and the psychrometric and curve functions of the EMS.
- **`accim.energyplus`**: `find_energyplus()`, `idd_path()` and `read_idf_version()`. The environment variable `ACCIM_ENERGYPLUS_DIR` gives the folder of an EnergyPlus installation that is not in the default folder.
- **`accim.constants`**: `SUPPORTED_ENERGYPLUS_VERSIONS` and `ENERGYPLUS_VERSIONS_WITH_SPACES`.
- **`accim.sim.accis.load_idf()`**, which reads an IDF with besos (`'auto'`) or with eppy and the IDD of a version.
- **`accim.parametric_and_optimisation.objectives.checked_sum_results()`**: a reducer for cumulative objectives (energy, for instance) that stops with a `ValueError` when the values of an evaluation are missing, empty, not numeric or not finite, or their sum overflows, instead of skipping the missing values as `sum_results()` does, with which an empty series (a failed simulation) gave an objective of 0 (from the tag `archive/feat-comfort-metrics-experiment-api`).
- **Comfort objectives independent of the control**: `objectives.hours_outside_comfort()` and `fraction_outside_comfort()` turn the adaptive comfort status that EnergyPlus reports for each `People` object (`Zone Thermal Comfort CEN 15251 Adaptive Model Category II Status`, `Zone Thermal Comfort ASHRAE 55 Adaptive Model 80% Acceptability Status`...: 1 inside the range, 0 outside, -1 when the zone is empty), which accim enables in all its models, into the occupied hours outside the range (hourly values) or their fraction of the occupied time. Unlike the comfort hours of accim, which follow the comfort model that accim controls, they measure every evaluation of an optimisation against the same model, also when it changes the coefficients of the custom model or the aPMV setpoints. The overview page of the parametric module shows how to read them.
- **Existing HVAC systems that keep the operative temperature between the setpoints** (script types `ex_ac` and `ex_mm`; new arguments of `AddAccis`, `add_accis_to_idf` and the classes of `accim.parametric_and_optimisation`, which do not change the model by default): `existing_hvac_availability_schedule` (`None`) gives a schedule (a schedule of the model, or `'On 24/7'`, which accim adds) to the HVAC objects that heat or cool the zones (zone equipment, air terminals, unitary systems, fans, coils, coil systems, VRF systems and evaporative coolers) and to the plant and condenser operation schemes objects with one scheme, and replaces the availability managers of their air and plant loops with an `AvailabilityManager:Scheduled` object with it, so that, with `'On 24/7'`, they are always available; `existing_hvac_widen_outdoor_temperature_limits` (`False`) widens the outdoor temperature limits of the HVAC objects (the outdoor temperatures outside which the compressors of the DX coils and VRF systems, or the supplemental heaters, do not work; for instance, the VRF systems of EnergyPlus do not cool above 43 °C or heat above 16 °C by default) to those of the VRF systems of accim, -20 and 80 °C, within the bounds of the IDD; and `existing_hvac_ideal_loads_backup` (`False`) adds to each occupied zone with zone HVAC equipment a backup ideal loads air system (`<zone> ACCIS Backup Ideal Loads`), the last equipment of the zone, which supplies the load to the setpoints that the existing HVAC system does not meet, whatever its type and the reason (capacity, controls, operating limits, availability, no equipment that cools or heats), with new outputs of the backups in the output types `'simplified'` and `'standard'` (`Zone Ideal Loads Zone Sensible Heating Rate` and `... Cooling Rate`). Without the first two, accim warns when the availability (schedules that are 0 at some time of the run period, or availability managers that can switch them off) or the outdoor temperature limits of the existing HVAC systems can switch them off; and, with `existing_hvac_radiant_setpoints=False` (see Changed), it warns about the HVAC objects that follow setpoint schedules of their own instead of the thermostats (radiant systems, ventilated slabs, cooling panels), which then do not follow the setpoints of accim. In a year in Seville, the sample OSM office (operation schedule from 6 to 19 h, zones occupied until 23 h) has 100 % of its occupied hours within the setpoints with `'On 24/7'` or with the backup (86 to 93 % without them), the sample model with electric radiators, which cannot cool, 99.9 to 100 % with the backup (27 to 32 % without it), and the example file `5ZoneAirCooled` of EnergyPlus (VAV with reheat) 100 % with the backup (93 to 100 % without it). Section 4.1.1 of the detailed use; the schedules of the HVAC systems and the values of the schedules of a model are in the new module `accim.sim.hvac.schedules`. Tests in `tests/core/test_existing_hvac.py`, and the configurations `b21` and `b22` of the golden master.

- **`population_size` of `run_optimisation()` with OMOPSO, SMPSO, CMAES, GeneticAlgorithm and EvolutionaryStrategy**: platypus takes the size of their generations from other options (`swarm_size` of the particle swarms, `offspring_size` of the others; 100 by default) and ignored `population_size` (the swarms and CMA-ES) or took it only as the initial population (GeneticAlgorithm and EvolutionaryStrategy), so these algorithms evaluated 100 solutions (EnergyPlus simulations) in each generation, whatever the `population_size`. It is now passed as `swarm_size` or `offspring_size` too, as the docstring says (unless `algorithm_options` gives them), so the optimisations with these algorithms run the planned number of simulations, and their results change. ### Added

### Fixed
- **Custom adaptive model (`ComfStand=99`) with EnergyPlus 26.1 and later**: the output IDFs have a new EMS program, `InitASTvariables`, called at `BeginNewEnvironment`, which sets `m`, `n`, `ACSTnoTol` and `AHSTnoTol` to 0. In each timestep, `SetComfTemp` reads `m` and `n` (custom model) and `CountHours_<zone>` reads `ACSTnoTol` and `AHSTnoTol` before `SetAST` and `SetASTnoTol` set them. Since EnergyPlus 26.1 (pull requests 11409 and 11551 of EnergyPlus), a variable read before it is set stops the simulation in the first timestep of each environment, so EnergyPlus 26.2 stopped with `Variable = 'N' used in expression has not been initialized`, also in the parametric and optimisation studies of the custom model; before, the variables kept the value of the previous pass. The results do not change: the first timestep of an environment is a warmup timestep, `ComfTemp` is only reported, and the setpoints are calculated after `m` and `n` are set.
- **`addAccis()` with `TempCtrl='pmv'`**: the output IDFs are generated again. In accim 0.8.0 and 0.8.1, `genIDF` stopped with `UnboundLocalError: cannot access local variable 'ComfStand_value'` before saving any of them, because its PMV branch appended the lines of a comfort model to the EMS program `SetAST` with variables of the loops of the temperature branch. With PMV control, the Fanger thermostat of each zone overrides the dry-bulb thermostat of the adaptive setpoints, so `SetAST` keeps only its 4 base lines (`SetpointAcc`, `m`, `n` and `run SetAST_Master`). As in 0.7.7, `ACST` and `AHST` are still calculated, with the default comfort model of `SetInputData` (`ComfStand` 1, `CAT` 1, `ComfMod` 2; with PMV, the `ComfStand`, `CAT` and `ComfMod` arguments are not used), for the EMS programs that count comfort hours.
- **`addAccis()` with `ComfStand=[0]` as the only standard**: in accim 0.8.0 and 0.8.1, `genIDF` stopped with `UnboundLocalError: cannot access local variable 'ComfMod_value'` when the setpoints of the Spanish CTE (`ComfStand` 0) were the only standard of a run with `TempCtrl='temperature'`, both in fully air-conditioned and in mixed-mode runs, because the branch of `ComfStand` 0 has no `ComfMod` loop. It now uses `ComfMod` 0, as `SetInputData` does; the lines of `SetAST` for `ComfStand` 0 do not depend on `ComfMod`. With another standard in the list, it already worked.
- **Occupant instances in models with `Space` objects**: EnergyPlus names the instance of a `People` object `<People Name>` when the object is assigned to a Space or to a Zone of one space, and `<Space Name> <People Name>` for each space when it is assigned to a SpaceList, a ZoneList or a Zone of several spaces. accim used `<Space Name> <People Name>` in all the models with `Space` objects, so EnergyPlus stopped with `Invalid Output:Variable or Output:Meter Index Key Name` (EMS sensors `RMOT`, `PMOT` and `Occ_count_*` of `addAccis()`, and the PMV and occupancy sensors of the aPMV setpoints). It also left out the zones without `Space` objects of the models that have them, and the remainder spaces; it now includes them, with a warning that recommends assigning every surface of the zone to a Space. `set_pmv_input_parameters()` did not change the `People` objects assigned to a zone of one space, and `get_people_names_for_ems()`, `get_people_hierarchy()`, `get_idf_hierarchy_with_people()` (which now include the default and remainder spaces) and the output discovery of the parametric module assumed the same names. In these cases, the names of the EMS objects and the keys of the dictionaries of the aPMV setpoints do not change.
- **Zones with several occupant instances** (a zone of several spaces, a SpaceList or ZoneList that covers several spaces of a zone, a remainder space, or several `People` objects in a zone): `addAccis()` created a `ThermostatSetpoint:DualSetpoint` `<Zone> Dual SP` for each instance, so EnergyPlus stopped with `Duplicate name found`, and the aPMV setpoints wrote the schedules of the zone once for each instance. Now such a zone is one target, named after the zone, whose occupancy is the `Zone People Occupant Count` of the zone and whose PMV, for the aPMV setpoints, is the average of the PMV of its instances weighted by their occupants (their simple average while the zone is unoccupied), as the PeopleAverage method of the Fanger thermostats. The models with one instance per zone, such as the OpenStudio models with one space per zone and the `People` objects assigned to a SpaceList, give the same output IDFs as before.
- **`Table` with zones of several occupant instances**: in the models with SpaceLists, `Table` found the occupied zones through the Space Types of OpenStudio (spaces whose Space Type is the name of the SpaceList), so it found none when the spaces have no Space Type, and it named a zone of several spaces after its first space and listed the other spaces as occupied zones without results. The outputs of the occupants of a zone with several instances (one per space, or per `People` object) were attributed to the zone once per instance with the same name, such as the running mean outdoor temperature, or not at all, and those of an instance whose name contains the name of another zone, such as `Space 2 People Block1:Zone1` in Block1:Zone2, could be taken for those of the other zone. Now `Table` finds the occupied zones as accim does for its EMS objects: a zone with several instances keeps its name, and the outputs of its occupants are merged into one column (their mean for the outputs that `Table` averages, such as the running mean outdoor temperature, PMV and PPD, and their sum for the others); a zone with one instance is named as before (after its space if it follows the conventions of OpenStudio); and `block_zone_hierarchy` (now documented) only needs the zones whose names do not have the pattern `block:zone`. The results of the models without `Space` objects and of the OpenStudio models with one space per zone do not change.
- **Zone and block totals of `Table` with OpenStudio models**: in the models whose zones `Table` names after their spaces (one space per zone, with the occupants in a SpaceList named like the Space Type of the spaces, as in OpenStudio):
  - the columns `<zone>_Total Energy Demand (kWh/m2) (summed)` and `<zone>_Total Energy Consumption (kWh/m2) (summed)` were 0, because `Table` looked for the outputs of the HVAC system of each zone with the name of the zone, after renaming the zone after its space;
  - when the names of the spaces have blanks, as the default names of OpenStudio (`Space 1`), the EMS outputs of the zones, whose names end with `<Space>_<People>` with the blanks replaced by `_`, did not get the block: their block totals were 0 (comfort hours, floor area and air volume) and the block totals of energy, divided by that floor area, were `inf`. These outputs kept their raw names, such as `EMS:COMFORTABLE HOURS_NO APPLICABILITY_SPACE_1_OCCUPANTS [H](HOURLY)`;
  - with `normalised_energy_units=True` (the default), `Table` stopped with `IndexError: list index out of range` when the names of the zones have no colon, as those of the sample `OSM_TestResidentialUnit_v01_onlygeometry` (`Floor_1_Zone`), because it looked for the floor area of each zone with the name of the zone.

  Now `Table` finds the outputs of each zone, and its floor area, with the name it gives the zone, and the EMS outputs of the zones named after spaces with blanks get the name of the space, so they get the block and are renamed after their zone, as those of the spaces without blanks already were (`BLOCK1:SPACE 1_Comfortable Hours_No Applicability (h)`). The results of the other models do not change.
- **Zones whose names contain the name of another zone in `Table`**: `Table` found the columns of each zone, block and excluded zone by the names they contain, so the zone totals of `Block1:Zone1` added up the outputs of `Block1:Zone10`, the block totals of `Block1` those of `Block10`, `level_excluded_zones=['Block1:Zone1']` also excluded `Block1:Zone10`, and, depending on the order of the zones, which changed from one Python process to another, the columns of two such zones could get the same names. Now `Table` takes the whole name of each zone and block. Also, in the models without `Space` objects whose zones are not named `block:zone` and have blanks (`Zone 1`), the EMS outputs of the zones did not get the block, so their block totals were 0, and `Table` stopped with `IndexError` with `normalised_energy_units=True`.

### Changed
- **Zone columns of `Table`**: with `normalised_energy_units=True` (the default), the energy of each zone (`<zone>_Cooling Energy Demand (kWh/m2)`, and the heating demand, the cooling and heating consumption and the zone totals `<zone>_Total Energy Demand (kWh/m2) (summed)` and `Total Energy Consumption`) is now divided by the floor area of the zone, as its units say. Before, it was the energy of the zone in kWh, except the zone totals of the zones whose names have no colon. For the sample `TestModel` in Seville (`vrf_ac`), `Block1:Zone1_Cooling Energy Demand (kWh/m2)` was 5136.95 and is now 341.38; the block and building totals do not change. And the EMS outputs of the zones are named after their zones in all the models, as they were in the OpenStudio models: `EMS:COMFORTABLE HOURS_NO APPLICABILITY_BLOCK1_ZONE1 [H](HOURLY)` is now `Block1:Zone1_Comfortable Hours_No Applicability (h)` (and so the floor area, the air volume, and the discomfort and ventilation hours), and the EMS outputs that `Table` does not rename have the name of the zone instead of the EMS suffix (`EMS:OCCUPIED HOURS_BLOCK1:ZONE1 [H](HOURLY)`). Code that reads these columns by their names, or the energy of the zones, needs to be updated.
- **Models without People objects**: `addAccis()` and the other functions that use `accimJob` stop with a `ValueError` that explains the problem when the model has no `People` objects (or `People:Instance` objects, in EnergyPlus 26.2), instead of an `UnboundLocalError` in `setComfFieldsPeople`.

  - The results have one row per row of the plan (per row and EPW when the plan has no `epw` column), in the order of the plan, also with `processes > 1`.   - With `keep_dirs=True`, `<out_dir>/BESOS_Output` is created before the BESOS evaluator, tolerating a folder created by another process. Before, processes starting at the same time could try to create it together, and one of them failed with `FileExistsError`.

- **aPMV setpoints applied on the aPMV scale (`apply_apmv_setpoints`)**: the EMS program `apply_aPMV_<target>` passed `s/(1 + lambda*s)` to the Fanger thermal comfort thermostat, where `s` is `pmv_heating_sp` or `pmv_cooling_sp` and `lambda` is the adaptive coefficient of the current season. That is the aPMV of a PMV equal to `s`, not the PMV whose aPMV equals `s`, so the zone aPMV settled around `s/(1 + 2*lambda*s)` and the effect of `lambda` was inverted.
  - `pmv_heating_sp` and `pmv_cooling_sp` are now targets on the aPMV scale, and the setpoint tolerances are applied on that scale.

  - `count_aPMV_comfort_hours_<target>` now compares the zone aPMV with these aPMV setpoints, so `Comfortable Hours_<target>` counts the hours within the setpoints that are actually applied.

## [0.7.6] - 2025-12-16

### Added
- **Robust ESO Results Parsing**: Added `read_eso_using_readvarseso` to `accim.utils`.
  - Uses the native EnergyPlus `ReadVarsESO` utility for 100% format compatibility.
  - Automatically separates data by reporting frequency (Hourly, Monthly, Timestep).
  - Generates metadata tables (Report Type, Area, Units) replicating DesignBuilder/ResultsViewer structure.
  - Intelligently filters out Design Days and Sizing Periods to return only the RunPeriod data.
- **Variable Key Pattern Identification**: Added `identify_variable_key_pattern` to `accim.utils`.
  - Automatically detects the naming convention (Key Index) used by EnergyPlus for specific report variables (e.g., returns placeholders like `[Zone Name]`, `[Space Name] [People Name]`, or `[Schedule Name]`).
  - Executes a rapid micro-simulation (1 day, minimal shadowing) to generate actual output keys.
  - Implements a robust two-level search strategy:
    1. **Direct Object Match**: Scans raw IDF fields to match keys against any object name (VRF, Schedules, Coils), ignoring specific IDD field names.
    2. **Hierarchy Match**: Analyzes Zone/Space/People relationships, supporting both modern (E+ 9.6+) and legacy hierarchies.
  - Handles variables with multiple keys and prioritizes specific object matches over global environment variables.
- **PMV Parameter Management**: Added `set_pmv_input_parameters` to `apmv_setpoints`.
  - Allows bulk modification of `People` objects (Activity Level, Clothing Insulation, Air Velocity, Work Efficiency).
  - Automatically creates and assigns the necessary `Schedule:Compact` objects.
  - Supports global values (float) or zone-specific values (dictionary).
- **Model Introspection Tools** in `accim.utils`:
  - `get_idf_hierarchy`: Extracts the relationship between Zones, Spaces, ZoneLists, and SpaceLists.
  - `get_people_hierarchy`: Resolves exactly which spaces are affected by specific `People` objects.
  - `get_available_target_names` and `get_input_template_dictionary`: Helpers to assist users in configuring simulation dictionaries with valid keys.
- **EMS Debugging**: Added `add_ems_debug_output` helper to easily enable Verbose EMS reporting in the `.edd` file.
- **Automatic Metering**: `apply_apmv_setpoints` now automatically adds `Output:Meter` objects for `EnergyTransfer:HVAC` and `Electricity:HVAC`.

### Changed
- **Major Refactor of `apply_apmv_setpoints`**:
  - **Unified Version Support**: Removed separate logic paths for legacy (< v23.1) and modern EnergyPlus versions. The code now dynamically detects the model structure.
  - **Hierarchy Resolution**: Implemented a strict 3-level priority logic (`Space/SpaceList` > `ZoneList` > `Zone`) to correctly identify EMS control targets.
  - **Dictionary Inputs**: Arguments like `adap_coeff_cooling` or `pmv_cooling_sp` now accept dictionaries, allowing different values for specific zones or spaces.
- **Infrastructure Generation**: The creation of Schedules and Thermostats is now smarter. It checks for existing objects and updates or replaces them (e.g., converting a standard DualSetpoint thermostat to a ThermalComfort one) without creating duplicates.
- **User Feedback**: Standardized `verbose_mode` across functions. Success messages are optional, but warnings for conflicts (e.g., duplicate objects) are now always displayed.

### Fixed
- **EMS Naming Issues**: Fixed `Invalid variable name` errors in EnergyPlus. A new sanitization function replaces invalid characters (hyphens, dots, spaces, parentheses) with underscores in EMS variable names.
- **EMS Sensor Mapping**: Fixed a critical bug where EMS Sensors pointed to the original `People` object name instead of the internal instance name generated by EnergyPlus when using `ZoneList` or `SpaceList`.
- **BESOS Compatibility**: Fixed runtime crashes when reading `.eso` files containing empty units `[]`, duplicate keys, or trailing schedule names. This is handled via a robust "Monkey Patch" applied automatically to `besos.objectives.read_eso`.
